O'Dowds Seafood Bar: A True Seafood Chowder in Connemara

August 2026 — Connemara, Ireland

A first for this blog — a true seafood chowder, slurped in a little seaside pub with a pint in one hand and my new wife across the table.

Live and slurp mollusk friends, a monumentous event has occurred in my life recently, my lifeforce has been inextricably joined with another on the perilous journey we call life, I now have a wife in other words.

We travelled to ireland to the beautiful belle isle castle, and I must say while I never thought I would do a destination wedding, I could not have been happier with it.

During our 3 day tenure at the castle with our guests, I found myself oddly alone, behind the castle, staring out at the pure blue lake across a pasture, and I thought to myself I may never feel this serene and at peace again.

I find that I feel most at peace in life just before an event where there really is no turning back, where you are poised at the edge of a cliff and you have no other choice but to dive right off of it, luckily for me in this case this was into the arms of my beautiful wife.

While staring out at the lake I was struck with a sudden fondness that I am sure you are all aware of, one for chowder in all its forms.

And that fondness dragged something else up with it.

I had forgotten this blog, in a way as all time consumes all so does it consume our memories, and as life got busier my memory had waned for this blog site, and I realized what a tremendous loss that was. Where else could I write my unadultered thoughts to my mollusk friends, with that I steeled myself and was determined that no matter how much revelry occurred at this wedding of mine (there was much) I would no longer shirk my duty to this blog site.

With that I promptly forgot about the blog site again as I was swept up in the whirlwind that is getting married, it was a night to remember, none of which will be shared here as those thoughts and memories must stay sacred for the people who experienced them, lest they become less sacred due to many people knowing them.

I digress and this is where we come to the chowder of things, a first for this blog I experienced a true seafood chowder. I had found that in ireland a 'clam chowder' was nowhere to be found, it was a profoundly new england item to have.

On our journey around ireland we stopped in connemara county, and by the seaside there was a nice little pub called O'Dowds seafood bar and restaurant. We went for a pint of Guinness as is customary, and I saw on the menu they had a Seafood Chowder, I had to try it for I remembered my blog site now, and a good thing I changed the blog site name to include all chowder.

I was sat in this pub with my beautiful wife, waiting on another love of mine that was chowder, and when it came out I was impressed.

It had a creamy orange color reminiscent of lobster bisque, and there was lobster aplenty awaiting me in the chowder, along with an unidentified white fish, salmon, potentially monkfish and oodles of flavor.

Unfortunately and oddly for ireland there was no potato, and that was a loss.

What did accompany this chowder instead of the usual crackers was brown bread, with a slight flavor of molasses, and this was a wonderful accompaniment to the chowder, albeit strange to one not of Ireland as I am.

The first taste of that chowder made me recoil, it was rich, so rich and tasteful I was curious if I could even finish it (I did), as I ate my way through the rich chowder and the beautiful brown bread soaked in said chowder, I realized that while life can be difficult at times, working, living, just being, the times at peace, where you are sat at an irish pub, with a pint and your beautiful wife, slurping down rich seafood chowder the likes that I am not sure is present in the U.S, makes the toil of it all worth it.

Live and slurp mollusk brethren, and may your chowder be always rich and your life as peaceful and serene as you wish it.

8.5/10 — due to no clam.
